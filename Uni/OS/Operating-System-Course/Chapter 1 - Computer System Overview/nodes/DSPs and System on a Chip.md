# DSPs and System on a Chip
**Problem:** Streaming signal workloads (audio/video) and handheld device constraints push specialized, tightly-integrated computation beyond a single general-purpose CPU.
**Mechanism:** DSPs deal with streaming signals such as audio or video; once embedded in I/O devices like modems, they are now becoming first-class computational devices, especially in handhelds. Other fixed-function units (codecs, encryption/security support) coexist alongside the CPU. To meet handheld requirements, the System on a Chip (SoC) puts the CPU(s), caches, DSPs, GPUs, I/O devices, and main memory all on the same chip.

## Key points
- [ ] DSPs deal with streaming signals such as audio or video and are becoming first-class computational devices, especially in handhelds. (p.11)
- [ ] Other specialized fixed-function units (e.g. codecs for encoding/decoding speech and video, encryption/security support) coexist with the CPU. (p.11)
- [ ] The SoC integrates CPUs, caches, DSPs, GPUs, I/O devices (codecs, radios), and main memory onto a single chip, replacing the classic standalone microprocessor for handheld devices. (p.12)
